# From OTFS to AFDM: Comparative Study of Next-Generation ISAC Waveforms in Dual-Dispersion Channels [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: It is anticipated that the fifth-generation (5G) and sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems will adopt extremely high frequency (EHF) technology, operating in millimeter wave (mmWave) and terahertz (THz) bands to support applications such as the Internet of Things (IoT), edge computing, and smart cities [1]. Additionally, scenarios like vehicle-to-everything (V2X), high-speed railways, and non-terrestrial networks (NTN) are expected to be impacted by heterogeneous and high mobility conditions [3]. High mobility scenarios pose significant challenges to wireless communication systems due to the dual-dispersive wireless channels known as time-varying (TV) multipath or time-frequency selective [4]. This heterogeneous scattering environment can degrade the received signal through path delay and Doppler shift, leading to intersymbol interference (ISI) and carrier-to-interference (CII), significantly reducing the communication performance under traditional efficient modulation schemes (e.g., Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) [5]. With this challenge, there is an increasing expectation that B5G and 6G systems will incorporate integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) capabilities, possibly with a unified hardware and signal processing technology [6]. In addition to providing environmental perception and accurate/reliable positioning information for the aforementioned applications, the enhanced features introduced by ISAC are crucial for improving spectrum and energy efficiency, as well as reducing the hardware cost of systems operating in high mobility scenarios [3].
Although it is difficult to predict which generation of the upcoming 5G/6G standards, namely B5 G or 6G- will see ISAC adoption and implementation in commercial systems, this topic has been one of the most intensely discussed topics in recent years before the wireless system standardization forum. Notable examples include the 6G Intelligence Alliance for Networks and Services (6G-IA) where ISAC has been identified as a priority technology for its members, and the 5G Automotive Association (5GAA). Here, ISAC is considered a support technology for Cellular-V2X (C-V2X) services. While it can be reasonably expected that any form of actual deployment of 5G-based ISAC could leverage OFDM, specifically using cyclic prefix (CP)-OFDM and Discrete Fourier Transform Extensions of OFDM (DFT-s-OFDM), for 6G, new waveforms such as Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) and Amplified Frequency Division Multiplexing (AFDM) should consider fully exploiting the potential of ISAC, which will be studied in this paper. In fact, important standardization bodies such as the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) and the Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) have recently added ISAC to their work plans and roadmaps. In November 2023, ETSI established a new group dedicated to ISAC.
Following this trend, novel waveforms have been proposed recently due to their ability to maintain symbol orthogonality under bichromatic dispersion conditions, making them robust for high mobility and beneficial for ISAC because they inherently enable estimation of environmental parameters such as the distance and velocity (i.e., delay and Doppler shift) of scattering objects. One of the most popular methods is OTFS signaling [7], which utilizes an inverse Sine FFT (ISFFT) to directly modulate information symbols on a 2D grid in the delay-Doppler domain, gaining significant attention from high mobility B5 G systems due to its superior performance compared to current used waveforms such as OFDM [8].
In fact, the full delay-Doppler representation inherently conveys information about the velocity and range of scatterers in OTFS channels, which is naturally communicated in the form of corresponding multipath delays and Doppler shifts. This means significant benefits for ISAC. Therefore, a large number of ISAC technologies based on OTFS have been proposed to directly extract the delay and Doppler parameters from channel state information (CSI), which has been proven to compete with OFDM and Frequency Modulation Continuous Wave (FMCW) radar in terms of detection performance, and is more robust to mobility and achievable capacity [9].
A recent idea to alleviate this latter drawback is the AFDM waveform, which utilizes Discrete Affine Fourier Inverse Transform (IDAFT) [12] to modulate information symbols into the distorted time-frequency domain, thereby generating the desired delay-Doppler orthogonality while maintaining necessary flexibility. The configurable parameters of AFDM also come with other desirable attributes, such as full diversity guarantee and increased throughput [11], making AFDM a strong candidate for ISAC enablement waveforms in B5G and 6G systems.
This paper aims to comprehensively analyze the basic principles and future of ISAC technology in heterogeneous high mobility scenarios, comparing prominent candidate waveforms in the form of OTFS and AFDM, with a focus on signal processing in both domains. The analysis shows that the novel delay-Doppler orthogonal design in OTFS and AFDM is conducive to signal processing in communication and sensing functions, advocating for their integration. These insights may not only be of great interest and value to academicians but also to standardization engineers involved in the development of future generations of mobile broadband systems across various vertical industries.

Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) using next-generation waveforms
In ISAC, wireless "sensing" refers to the capability inherently embedded in radio signals affected by channel conditions to provide rich information about the surrounding environment. The ISAC technology using a single waveform 16 can be divided into two categories: a) communication-centric design - aimed at achieving sensing functionality through primary communication waveforms (OFDM, OTFS, AFDM, etc.) by extracting environmental/target information from received signals; b) radar-centric design - aimed at achieving communication functionality through primary radar waveforms (pulses, FMCW, etc.) by embedding information into modulated signals or through dual-function joint design, which aims to achieve both functionalities through novel designed waveforms that are not specific to any of the ISAC functions. This paper investigates the communication-centric design method in bi-color dispersion channels using OFDM, OTFS, and AFDM communication waveforms to achieve sensing functionality.

Then, compared with mature radar technologies [27], two types of sensing problems can be identified: 1) detection problem (DP), which involves distinguishing between the number of unique scattering points (targets) in the background clutter from the interest, and 2) estimation problem (EP), which involves extracting distance, velocity, and orientation associated with the target. Such sensing scenarios can be categorized as either monostatic, where the sensing receiver is co-located with the transmitter, or bistatic, where the sensing receiver is spatially far away from the transmitter.

Comparative analysis centered on key performance indicators (KPIs)
Given the above, we have finally provided a qualitative comparison of OFDM, OTFS, and AFDM - representing the classics of B5 G/6 G systems, SotA, and the latest alternative - ISAC-friendly waveforms. To this end, we considered various relevant KPIs for communication and sensing, both functionally and in implementation. This includes comparing GFDM and FMCW waveforms for their performance in communication and radar sensing respectively.
The results are presented in Table 1, although due to space limitations, it is not possible to elaborate on all comparison points. However, we briefly outline some of the most important selected key performance indicators. Specifically, perhaps the most critical communication KPI in bi-dispersive channels is Doppler frequency shift robustness, i.e., compatibility with high mobility and EHF conditions due to inherent delay-Doppler orthogonality, which is only achieved by OTFS and AFDM waveforms. In this regard, it is noteworthy that OTFS achieves full diversity only when converging to a first-order asymptotic diversity mechanism at finite SNR [22], while AFDM generally provides guaranteed full diversity [11] and is also known to be the only Doppler-robust waveform in MIMO scenarios. On the other hand, due to their fundamental roots being OFDM, both AFDM and GFDM suffer from higher Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR), while OTFS enjoys low PAPR due to its DFT-based extension of symbol power in the time-frequency domain. This advantage is closely related to implementation costs and hardware stability, especially concerning the efficiency of power amplifiers (PA) and radio frequency (RF) components, which become more pronounced in large-scale MIMO scenarios.
Another important consideration is computational complexity, which is related to various signal processing processes (e.g., modulation and channel estimation). In this regard, although OTFS and AFDM can both be interpreted as pre-coding schemes for OFDM transmitters, allowing the core OFDM modulator to be reused, a one-dimensional (1D) AFDM modulator exhibits higher efficiency than a two-dimensional (2D) OTFS modulator. This reduced dimensionality in waveforms also demonstrates similar advantages for channel estimation in terms of computational complexity and required pilot overhead.

In terms of target detection performance, OTFS and AFDM show significant improvements in the Doppler domain ambiguity of OFDM, but such methods are limited because they do not optimize for correlation like FMCW waveforms. Therefore, by utilizing super-resolution methods based on discrete delay-Doppler domain and grid estimation techniques, it is possible to achieve extremely high resolution and period with sufficient parameterization (e.g., carrier frequency and symbols), compared to FMCW commonly used in automotive radar.
The above attributes in communication and detection performance must be satisfied and be coherent to make waveforms strong candidates for ISAC in B5G/6G. Clearly, as observed from the color scaling in Table I, OTFS and AFDM are the most promising candidates to meet most ISAC standards, with some compromises in complexity, power, and spectral efficiency between the two waveforms, which will be further addressed in future work.
Future Work
It is evident that although OTFS and AFDM are the most promising candidates for realizing high-performance ISAC in next-generation wireless networks, there are still many important topics to be addressed so that these technologies can be incorporated into future standards. In fact, any given row in Table I (such as PAPR of AFDM or CSI estimation complexity of OTFS) could be a topic for optimization and development. The authors hope this paper will help the ISAC research community provide basic insights, technical directions, and future trends for the development of high-performance ISAC in dual-dispersion environments in next-generation wireless networks. Furthermore, given the solid foundations identified and the potential and challenges identified in the dual-dispersion environment and promising waveforms, it is also important to investigate the impacts on subsequent practical implementation and design.
